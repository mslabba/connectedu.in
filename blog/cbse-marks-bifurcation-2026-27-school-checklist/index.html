CBSE Marks Bifurcation 2026-27: A Practical and Internal Assessment Checklist for Schools

10 Nov 2026 · Exams · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

On 6 November 2026, CBSE put up a corrected circular giving the subject-wise marks bifurcation for Class X and Class XII, Examination 2026-27. For every subject it lists the maximum marks for theory, practical, project and internal assessment (IA), the length of the theory paper, whether an external examiner will be appointed for the practical or project, whether the Board supplies practical answer books, and the type of theory answer book.

CBSE's reason is plain. As NDTV reported (6 November 2026), the Board has seen schools make mistakes while uploading practical, project and IA marks. This post turns the circular into a checklist for the school office and the exam in-charge: set up each subject the way the Board does, record marks against the right component, and check them before anything goes to CBSE. It is written for principals, exam coordinators and office staff of CBSE schools.

What the 6 November circular says

  • Every subject is out of 100, split between theory and the internal components listed for that subject.
  • The annex covers each subject code with: theory duration, maximum marks for theory, practical, project and IA, whether an external examiner is appointed, whether practical answer books come from the Board, and the theory answer book type (for example 32 pages, 20 pages, or graph pages for Class X Mathematics Standard).
  • The schedule for practicals, projects and IA is in two phases: winter-bound schools in November–December 2026, ending 19 December 2026, and regular-session schools from 1 January to 13 February 2027. CBSE re-uploaded the circular on 6 November to correct the regular-session end date, which the first upload had printed wrongly, as TeachCBSE noted.

Read the official circular PDF on cbse.gov.in for your own subject codes. It is listed on CBSE's examination circulars page under 06/11/2026.

Two things stand out. In Class X, the annex shows no external examiner for any subject, so the school runs and marks every internal component itself. In Class XII, the practical and project subjects do get an external examiner, so the school has to have candidate lists, batches and records ready on the day the examiner arrives.

Also notice that the same 20 marks are called different things: IA in Class XII Mathematics, project in Class XII Accountancy. If your marks register has one column called "internal" for every subject, that is exactly the kind of mismatch the circular is trying to stop.

Step 1: set up each subject to match the annex

Before a single practical is held, the exam in-charge should make one sheet (or one exam setup in your school software) per class:

  1. List every subject code each section offers, taken from your LOC data. If you are still fixing LOC entries, see our CBSE LOC checklist for the school office.
  2. Against each code, copy the maximum marks for theory, practical, project and IA from the annex. Leave a component blank when the annex shows a dash.
  3. Check every row adds up to 100.
  4. Mark which subjects have an external examiner and which get Board-supplied practical answer books, so the office knows what to expect from CBSE and what it must arrange itself.
  5. Have a second teacher check the sheet against the PDF and sign it. Most upload errors start here, not at the upload screen.

Step 2: record marks against the right component

  • Enter each mark under its own component. A Class XII Physics student has a practical mark out of 30; a Class XII History student has a project mark out of 20; a Class X Science student has an IA mark out of 20. Do not fold them into one "internal" figure.
  • Never let a mark exceed its maximum. A practical mark of 32 in a 30-mark practical is the kind of error that only shows up when someone adds it up.
  • Record absence separately from zero. A student who missed the practical is not the same as a student who scored nothing, and the office will need to know which is which.
  • Keep the source behind each mark: practical record books, project files, IA test papers or rubrics, and the attendance sheet for each practical batch.

Step 3: plan the practical window

  • Winter-bound schools are inside the window now and must finish by 19 December 2026. If you have not fixed batches and dates, do it this week.
  • Regular-session schools have 1 January to 13 February 2027. Use November and December to finish projects, settle batches and confirm lab readiness, so January is about conducting, not chasing.
  • Class X students sit CBSE's two board exams from 2026-27; internal assessment still needs to be complete and checked before you upload.
  • Make sure each candidate in a practical batch matches the LOC, including name and date of birth. Our post on CBSE name and DOB corrections covers how to fix those before the marksheet, not after.

Step 4: check before you upload

  • Print or export the class-wise marks list and have the subject teacher sign it.
  • Spot-check five students per subject against their record books or project files.
  • Confirm no blank cells where a mark is due, and no marks where the annex shows a dash.
  • Keep a dated copy of what was uploaded, and note who uploaded it.
  • If a mark is changed after sign-off, write down who changed it, when and why. Our audit log post explains why schools want this on record when a parent or the Board asks later.

Common mistakes the circular is trying to stop

  • Using last year's split for a subject whose components changed
  • Putting a Class XII project mark in an IA column, or the other way round
  • Entering a practical mark above its maximum
  • Treating an absent student as zero, or zero as absent
  • Uploading marks for a student whose LOC details still have an error
  • No signed sheet, so nobody can say which mark list was final

Frequently asked questions

What is CBSE marks bifurcation 2026-27?

It is CBSE's subject-wise split of the 100 marks in each Class X and XII subject between theory, practical, project and internal assessment, published in a circular corrected and re-uploaded on 6 November 2026.

How are Class 10 Science and Maths marks split?

80 marks for theory and 20 for internal assessment, for Science (086) and for both Mathematics Standard (041) and Basic (241), as per the annex.

How are Class 12 Physics, Chemistry and Biology marks split?

70 marks for theory and 30 for the practical, with an external examiner appointed and practical answer books supplied by the Board.

When are CBSE practicals for 2026-27?

Winter-bound schools hold them in November and December 2026, ending 19 December 2026. Regular-session schools hold them from 1 January to 13 February 2027.

Will an external examiner come for Class 10 practicals?

The annex shows no external examiner for any Class X subject. Class XII practical and project subjects do get one.

Is this official CBSE guidance?

No. It summarises CBSE's 6 November 2026 circular and news reports of it for school-office planning. Always check the circular PDF and CBSE's own instructions before conducting assessments or uploading marks.

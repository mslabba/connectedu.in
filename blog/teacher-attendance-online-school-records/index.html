Teacher Attendance Is Going Online: What Principals and School Offices Should Keep in Their Own Records

16 Oct 2026 · Staff attendance

Book a Demo School Login

If your state has ordered online teacher attendance, then yes, you have to record it in the state's system, and the headmaster or principal is usually the person answerable for it. That doesn't replace your school's own staff records. The state app shows whether a teacher was marked present. Your office still has to deal with late arrivals, half days, leave and salary, under rules that are specific to your school.

Here is what has changed, who is responsible, and what the office should keep for itself.

What has actually changed?

Two states have made teacher attendance a digital, daily requirement this year.

Maharashtra. From 15 October 2026, schools across the state have been told to record the attendance of all teachers from Class 1 to Class 12 online through the state's Smart Attendance system. The direction covers schools of all mediums and managements, and headmasters are responsible for making sure attendance is recorded every day. Attendance is tracked through the state's Vidya Samiksha Kendra (VSK). Student attendance is already being recorded online from this academic year. (Hindustan Times, 14 Oct 2026)

Karnataka. The Department of School Education and Literacy has launched Kartavya-KAAMS (Karnataka Advanced Attendance Management System), a facial-recognition mobile app with AI and GIS features. Permanent teaching and non-teaching staff must mark attendance on it twice a day from their place of duty from the 2026-27 academic year. The department has said the reports can be used for teacher deployment, staff rationalisation and salary payments. (The Hindu, 11 May 2026)

Other states may follow. The details, such as which schools and which staff are covered and which app is used, differ from state to state, so read the circular that applies to your school.

Do we now have to record teacher attendance online?

Check three things in your state's circular:

  1. Which schools it covers. Maharashtra's direction covers all mediums and managements. Karnataka's KAAMS order is about permanent staff of the school education department.
  2. Which staff it covers. Teachers only, or non-teaching staff as well? Permanent staff only, or contract staff too?
  3. How and when attendance is marked. Daily, or twice a day? Through which app or portal? From the school premises?

If your school is covered, the state system is where the official daily mark goes. Do not skip it in favour of your own register.

Who is responsible?

In Maharashtra, the order names the headmaster as responsible for daily recording. In practice, the principal usually hands the routine work to someone in the office, but the responsibility stays with the head of school. That means:

  • Someone named in the office checks every morning that every teacher's attendance has been marked.
  • The principal knows who to call when the app is down or a teacher can't mark.
  • There is a written way to handle exceptions: on-duty, training, exam duty and leave.

In Karnataka, each staff member marks their own attendance on the app, but the school still has to account for staff who are on training, exam evaluation duty or leave.

What does the state app not do for your school?

A state attendance system answers one question for the department: was this person present at their place of duty? Your school office has other questions to answer:

  • Late arrivals. Was the teacher on time, and does your policy turn repeated lates into a half day or leave?
  • Leave. What kind of leave was it, was it approved, and what balance is left?
  • Non-teaching and contract staff. These staff may fall outside the state order, but they are still on your payroll.
  • Salary and deductions. Many private and unaided schools work out pay and deductions from their own attendance records.
  • Your own history. If there is a dispute, a transfer or an inspection, you want your records in your own hands.

That is why the office should keep its own staff attendance record next to the state one.

What should the office keep in its own records?

At minimum, keep these for every staff member, every working day:

  • Date, staff name and staff ID
  • In-time (and out-time, if your school tracks it)
  • Status: present, late, half day, absent, on leave, on duty
  • Leave type and approval, where relevant
  • Reason or note for exceptions such as training, exam duty or a device failure
  • Who marked or corrected the entry, and when

Then write down your late and absent rules and apply them the same way every month. For example, a school might decide that 3 lates in a month count as a half day, and 6 lates count as one day of leave. The exact numbers are your school's call. What matters is that the rule is written, shared with staff, and applied consistently.

Checklist for principals and school offices

  • Read your state's circular and note which schools, staff and timings it covers.
  • Name one person in the office who checks the state app every morning.
  • Set up a backup step for when the app or network fails, and note it in your own record.
  • Keep your own daily staff attendance record, including non-teaching and contract staff.
  • Write down your late and absent rules (for example, 3 lates = half day, 6 lates = 1 day leave) and share them with staff.
  • Record leave type and approval with each absence.
  • Record on-duty, training and exam-duty days so they are not counted as absences.
  • Each month, compare your own record with the state record and resolve any differences.
  • Use your own record, with the rules applied, for salary and deductions.
  • Keep a log of who changed an entry and why.

Frequently asked questions

Is online teacher attendance mandatory for all schools?

It depends on your state's order. In Maharashtra, the direction covers schools of all mediums and managements for Classes 1 to 12, from 15 October 2026. In Karnataka, Kartavya-KAAMS is mandatory for permanent teaching and non-teaching staff of the school education department from 2026-27. Check the circular for your state.

Who is responsible for recording teacher attendance?

In Maharashtra, the headmaster is responsible for making sure teachers' attendance is recorded daily. Most schools hand the daily check to the office, but the responsibility stays with the head of school.

If we use the state app, do we still need our own staff attendance record?

Yes. The state app records presence for the department. Your school still needs its own record for late rules, leave, non-teaching and contract staff, and salary.

Does ConnectEdu connect to Smart Attendance or Kartavya-KAAMS?

No. ConnectEdu records staff attendance for the school's own use, through manual entry or biometric devices. Where the state requires its app, mark attendance there as well.

Can we set our own rules for late arrivals?

Yes. In ConnectEdu, late and absent rules can be set to match your school's policy, for example 3 lates in a month as a half day and 6 lates as one day of leave.

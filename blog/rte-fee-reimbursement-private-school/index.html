RTE Fee Reimbursement for Private Schools: The Student Records You Need to Claim It

5 Nov 2026 · Fees and compliance · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

For an RTE fee reimbursement claim under section 12(1)(c), a private school usually needs the same pack every time: a bank account in the school's name, each child's Aadhaar and APAAR ID, the tuition / uniform / textbook amounts claimed, and attendance that can prove the child was still on the roll. Bihar's November 2026 Gyandeep SOP put hard dates on that pack — upload by 10 November, mark promote or drop-out by attendance by 15 November, and expect an on-site check where officers ask parents whether any fee was charged and whether free dress and books were given. The order is Bihar-only. The checklist is useful anywhere a state reimburses the 25% RTE quota.

Private schools already know the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, section 12(1)(c): admit children from economically weaker and disadvantaged groups, and the state reimburses the prescribed fee. The gap that trips offices is rarely the law. It is the student file. When the portal window opens, the school that can pull one clean record per child finishes the upload; the school that has fees in one ledger, attendance in a register and Aadhaar in a WhatsApp folder does not.

Bihar's November 2026 deadlines at a glance

According to Prabhat Khabar (4 November 2026), the Bihar Primary Education Directorate has set a stage-wise SOP for private schools claiming reimbursement for EWS/DG children admitted under RTE. In summary:

By whenWhoWhat
10 NovPrivate schoolEnter on the Gyandeep portal: school details, a bank account in the school's name, and per child — tuition, uniform and textbook amounts plus Aadhaar and APAAR ID
15 NovPrivate schoolFor recommended children from sessions 2019-20 to 2023-24, and online-enrolled children for 2024-25 and 2025-26, mark promote or drop-out on the portal based on attendance
30 NovDistrict education officeUpload the district-committee recommended action on Gyandeep

The article is clear on two hard rules for this round: no Aadhaar or APAAR for a child means no reimbursement for that child, and reimbursement goes only to a bank account in the school's name — not a personal account. There is no leniency flagged for missing IDs this time.

What the SOP asks the school to upload

Think of the portal fields as two lists: school-level and child-level.

School-level

  • School profile details as required on Gyandeep
  • Bank account held in the school's name (not the principal's, not a trustee's personal account)

Per child

  • Tuition fee amount claimed
  • Uniform amount claimed
  • Textbook amount claimed
  • Aadhaar number
  • APAAR ID

Those five child fields are what most offices scramble for in the last week. Tuition, uniform and books should match what the school actually claims under the state's reimbursement rules for that session — do not invent figures here, and do not copy a neighbour school's sheet. Aadhaar and APAAR need to be on file before the upload window if you want every child paid.

Promote or drop-out by attendance — by 15 November

The second school deadline is attendance-driven. For older recommended cohorts (2019-20 through 2023-24) and for online-enrolled children in 2024-25 and 2025-26, the school must mark each child as promoted or dropped out on Gyandeep, based on attendance.

That only works if attendance was marked through the year, not reconstructed from memory in November. A named register, or a digital attendance history that can be shown class by class, is what a field officer will expect to see. The same discipline that protects a Class 10 or 12 child under the CBSE 75% attendance rule also protects an RTE reimbursement claim: if the portal asks "was this child still here?", you need a dated answer.

What inspectors ask parents on site

The district process is not only a portal upload. Per the same Prabhat Khabar report:

  • A district-level committee chaired by the District Magistrate oversees the process
  • Each nominated field officer is assigned at most one or two private schools
  • Officers do an on-site check of school records
  • They speak to students and parents
  • They ask whether the school charged any tuition fee
  • They ask whether free dress and books were actually given
  • They record parent statements

So the claim pack is not only what you typed into Gyandeep. It is also what your fee ledger, stock issue register and parent conversations will support when someone walks into the office. If parents were charged for something the school also claimed as reimbursable, or if free uniform and books were not issued, the on-site step will surface it.

The four payment stages (Bihar)

The article describes reimbursement in four stages:

  1. School upload — child details and school bank account on Gyandeep
  2. Physical verification — district office downloads the data; field officers verify on site and report to the district committee
  3. DM committee recommend — the DM-chaired committee reviews the verification report and recommends payment
  4. Directorate → NIC Patna → PFMS DBT — verified data goes from the Primary Education Directorate to NIC Patna, then Direct Benefit Transfer via PFMS into the school bank account

Knowing the path helps set expectations inside the school: upload is necessary, but payment follows verification and a committee recommendation. Missing IDs or a personal bank account stop the chain before DBT.

A record pack to keep ready (even outside Bihar)

Bihar's dates are local. The file you need is not. Before any state RTE reimbursement window, private-school accounts and office teams can keep this pack per RTE child:

  1. Identity — Aadhaar; APAAR / One Nation One Student ID where the state asks for it
  2. Admission proof — RTE 12(1)(c) admission category clear on the child's file
  3. Fee heads claimed — tuition, and where allowed, uniform and textbooks, with amounts that match the claim
  4. Issue proof for free items — if you claim uniform or books, keep a signed issue record that the child received them
  5. Attendance history — enough to answer promote / continue / drop-out without guessing
  6. School bank account — in the institution's name, with IFSC and account proofs ready for the portal
  7. Parent acknowledgements — a short note that no prohibited fee was collected for that child, if your state expects it

Keep one single student record rather than five folders. When Aadhaar sits in admissions, APAAR in a UDISE sheet, fees in a ledger and attendance in a register, every claim window becomes a reconciling exercise. When they sit on one profile, the office prints or exports a pack and uploads.

Clear fee structures and instalments, a clean fee receipt format and a written late fee policy also help: they make it obvious which heads were charged to paying students and which were not charged to RTE children. Online collection with reminders and UPI is for the fee-paying cohort; for RTE children the discipline is the opposite — do not collect what the Act and your state rules say must be free, and keep proof that free items were issued.

Frequently asked questions

What is RTE fee reimbursement for a private school?

Under RTE Act 2009 section 12(1)(c), private schools admit a share of children from economically weaker and disadvantaged groups. The state reimburses the prescribed fee. The exact portal, rates and documents are set by each state — Bihar currently routes claims through Gyandeep under a Directorate SOP.

What did Bihar ask private schools to enter by 10 November 2026?

School details, a bank account in the school's name, and for each child: tuition, uniform and textbook amounts plus Aadhaar and APAAR ID on the Gyandeep portal, as reported by Prabhat Khabar on 4 November 2026.

What if a child has no Aadhaar or APAAR?

For this Bihar round, the coverage says there is no reimbursement for that child, and no leniency on missing IDs.

Why does attendance matter for the claim?

By 15 November, Bihar private schools must mark older recommended cohorts and recent online-enrolled RTE children as promoted or dropped out on Gyandeep based on attendance. Without a reliable attendance history, that step is guesswork — and guesswork fails an on-site check.

Can reimbursement be paid into the principal's personal account?

Not under this Bihar SOP. Payment is described as PFMS DBT into a bank account in the school's name only.

Is this legal advice?

No. It summarises a public news report of a Bihar Primary Education Directorate SOP. Follow your own state education department's circulars and take counsel for your school's case.

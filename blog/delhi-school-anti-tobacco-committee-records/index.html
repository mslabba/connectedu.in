Delhi School Anti-Tobacco Committee: The Records to Keep Under the DoE SOP

9 Nov 2026 · Compliance · ConnectEdu Team

Book a Demo School Login

In the first week of November 2026, Delhi's Directorate of Education told every government, government-aided and recognised private unaided school to put a drug-free campus standard operating procedure in place. Most coverage focused on the committees and the awareness sessions. For the school office, the line that matters most comes near the end: schools must keep records of what they do, and send an implementation checklist to the district every month.

This post sets out what the SOP asks for, as reported by ETGovernment (2 Nov 2026), ABP Live (3 Nov 2026) and EdexLive (5 Nov 2026), and turns it into a simple records file a principal can hand to a nodal teacher. It is written for school leaders and admins. It is not legal or clinical advice. Read the Directorate's circular itself and follow your district office on the exact formats.

What the Delhi DoE SOP asks schools to do

According to ETGovernment (2 November 2026), the circular came from the DoE's Educational and Vocational Guidance and Counselling Bureau under the Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan and the Nasha Mukt Delhi Campaign. Its aims are preventive education, early identification, counselling and referral. In short, each school must:

  1. Form or strengthen an Institution Level Task Force (ITF) / Anti-Tobacco Committee, headed by the principal or head of school
  2. Strengthen the Prahari Club, which will run as the school-level Nasha Mukt Delhi Club with activities through the session
  3. Run NAVCHETNA Life Skills Module sessions for Classes VI to XII between the 15th and 17th of every month, taken by teachers trained in 2025-26
  4. Hold teacher and parent sensitisation programmes at least twice a session, preferably in August and January
  5. Set up a confidential referral route to health professionals, mental-health experts or de-addiction services for students suspected of or identified as using substances, without stigma
  6. Comply with the NDPS Act, 1985 and COTPA, 2003, plus other applicable instructions
  7. Keep records and send a monthly implementation checklist to the district authorities

District monitoring committees under the Deputy Director of Education (District) will make random inspections, review compliance reports and check that schools keep their information up to date on the Nasha Mukt Vidyalaya portal, as reported by ETGovernment and ABP Live (3 November 2026).

Who sits on the anti-tobacco committee

The reported membership of the ITF / Anti-Tobacco Committee:

Member Practical note for the office
Principal / head of school (chair) Signs off minutes and the monthly checklist
Nodal teacher or Prahari Club in-charge Usually the person who keeps the file day to day
Health and Wellness Ambassador Often already trained under school health programmes
School Management Committee member Record the name and the date they were nominated
Student representative Note class and the term they serve
Local police representative Keep the beat officer's or station's contact on the file

Put the committee list, with names, roles, phone numbers and the date it was formed, on the first page of the file. When the committee changes — a teacher transfers, a student passes out — update the page and date the change. An inspector's first question is usually "who is on it?"

The records file: what to keep, section by section

The SOP, as reported, lists the records schools must keep: committee meetings, Prahari Club activities, awareness programmes, life-skills sessions, referrals and student participation. Here is one way to lay out the file so the monthly checklist almost fills itself.

1. Committee meetings

  • Date, members present, agenda, decisions, who does what by when
  • Signed minutes, kept in date order
  • Any follow-up from the previous meeting marked done or carried forward

2. Prahari Club / Nasha Mukt Delhi Club activities

  • Activity name, date, classes involved, teacher in charge
  • Number of students who took part
  • A photo or two, with no student faces where a child could be identified, if your school policy prefers that

3. NAVCHETNA life-skills sessions (Classes VI–XII)

  • A monthly row per section: date (within the 15th–17th window), trained teacher, topic or module unit, students present
  • A note if a session moved because of an exam or a holiday, and when it was held instead

EdexLive (5 November 2026) and ETGovernment both say these sessions are monthly and fixed to the 15th–17th, so put twelve dates on the school calendar now rather than finding the window each month.

4. Teacher and parent sensitisation

  • Two sessions per academic session — plan them for August and January as the SOP prefers
  • Date, speaker or resource person, audience, number attending
  • A copy of the invite you sent to parents and the channel you used

5. Referrals (keep this part confidential)

  • This is the most sensitive section. The SOP stresses confidentiality and no stigma.
  • Keep referral details in a separate, locked file held by the counsellor or the person the principal names, not in the general committee folder
  • For the committee file and the monthly checklist, a count is usually enough: number of referrals made this month, and to which type of service — no names

If you already follow a policy for counsellor case notes, use it here. Do not put a child's referral details into a shared drive, a staff WhatsApp group or any tool that teachers across the school can open.

6. Tobacco-free campus basics

Delhi's SOP sits on top of older national rules that many schools already follow. Under Section 6(b) of COTPA, 2003, tobacco products may not be sold within 100 yards of an educational institution, as the Ministry of Health's PIB note restates. The MoHFW Guidelines for Tobacco Free Educational Institutions ask schools to put up "Tobacco Free Educational Institution" signs inside and "Tobacco Free Area" signs outside, name Tobacco Monitors (with their names and numbers on the signs), mark the 100-yard area with local authorities, and include "no tobacco use" in the code of conduct. The Ministry of Education's Nasha Mukt Vidyalaya portal is where schools record this.

For the file: a photo of each sign with the date it went up, the list of monitors, and any letter you sent to the local authority about shops inside the 100-yard zone.

7. The monthly checklist

Keep a copy of every checklist you send to the district, with the date sent and who sent it. If the portal shows a submission number or a screenshot, keep that too. When sections 1–6 are kept up each month, the checklist is a summary, not a scramble.

A month on one page

When What Who
1st week Committee meeting (or as your committee decides); minutes signed Principal, nodal teacher
15th–17th NAVCHETNA sessions, Classes VI–XII; attendance noted per section Trained teachers
Through the month Prahari Club activity; referral count updated confidentially Club in-charge, counsellor
Month end Implementation checklist to district; Nasha Mukt Vidyalaya portal updated Nodal teacher, principal signs
August and January Teacher and parent sensitisation sessions Committee

Common gaps an inspection will notice

  • A committee formed on paper with no minutes after the first meeting
  • NAVCHETNA dates that fall outside the 15th–17th window with no note of why
  • Parent sessions announced but no record of how many came
  • Referral details sitting in a shared folder or group chat
  • Signs put up years ago, faded, with a monitor who has since left
  • A portal that shows last term's data

None of these needs software to fix. They need one named person and a monthly habit.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Delhi anti-tobacco SOP apply to private schools?

Yes. As reported by ETGovernment on 2 November 2026, the DoE directed all government, government-aided and recognised private unaided schools in Delhi to implement it.

Who heads the anti-tobacco committee?

The principal or head of school. Members include the nodal teacher or Prahari Club in-charge, the Health and Wellness Ambassador, an SMC member, a student representative and a local police representative.

When do NAVCHETNA sessions happen?

Every month between the 15th and 17th, for Classes VI to XII, taken by teachers trained in the 2025-26 session.

How often are parent and teacher sessions needed?

At least twice each academic session, preferably in August and January.

What records does the SOP ask for?

Records of committee meetings, Prahari Club activities, awareness programmes, life-skills sessions, referrals and student participation, plus a monthly implementation checklist to the district authorities.

Who checks compliance?

District monitoring committees led by the Deputy Director of Education (District). They make random inspections, review compliance reports and check that schools keep the Nasha Mukt Vidyalaya portal up to date.

Is this legal or medical advice?

No. It summarises public news reports of the DoE circular and national ToFEI guidelines for school-office planning. Follow the circular, your district office and qualified professionals for any decision about a student.

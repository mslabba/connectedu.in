School Fee Receipt Format for Indian Schools: Show Tuition on Its Own Line

6 Oct 2026 · School fee receipt format

Book a Demo School Login

Short answer: A school fee receipt should show the school's name, address and affiliation number, a unique receipt number, payment date, student name, class and admission number, payer's name, fee period, each fee head with tuition on its own line, the total, payment mode and transaction reference, and a signature or system-generated note.

The tuition line matters most. Every January and February, salaried parents need fee proof for their employer. If the receipt just says "School fees: ₹42,500", they can't tell HR how much was tuition. So they come back to the office for a new receipt, and a few hundred of those requests make for a busy week.

General information, not tax advice. Parents should check their own situation with a tax adviser.

Why the tuition line matters to parents

Indian income-tax law lets an individual claim a deduction for tuition fees paid for the full-time education of up to two children at a university, college, school or other educational institution in India. The law specifically excludes development fees, donations and payments of a similar nature. The deduction sits inside the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit it shares with PPF, life insurance premiums, ELSS and similar items.

For years this was called Section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 is in force. The same deduction now sits in Section 123, with tuition fees listed in Schedule XV. The conditions, the two-child limit and the ₹1.5 lakh ceiling are the same. Most parents and HR teams will keep saying "80C" for a while, so it helps to know both names.

What this means in practice:

  • Only tuition counts. Development fees, donations, transport and other non-tuition heads are generally not eligible. If they're mixed into one total, the parent has no clean figure to claim.
  • The parent who pays claims it. The deduction goes to the individual who paid for their own child, so the receipt should show who paid.
  • Old tax regime only. The default new regime (Section 202 of the 2025 Act, which replaced Section 115BAC) doesn't allow this deduction. Parents who choose the old regime will still ask for these receipts.

What employers ask for (Form 12BB, now Form 124)

Salaried parents declare deductions to their employer so the right tax (TDS) is deducted from their salary. That declaration was Form 12BB. From tax year 2026-27 it is Form No. 124 (Rule 205, Income-tax Rules, 2026). The Income Tax Department's FAQ says it can include children's education fees and must come with supporting evidence. It goes to the employer, not the tax portal.

HR or payroll teams usually want:

  • a receipt or annual statement that names the child and the school,
  • the tuition amount shown clearly, separate from other heads,
  • dates showing the payment fell within the tax year (1 April to 31 March), and
  • a receipt they can trust: numbered, dated, and signed or marked as system-generated.

Each employer sets its own proof rules, but a clearly itemised receipt meets most of them.

What a good school fee receipt contains

Use this standard for every receipt, printed at the counter or generated after an online payment:

  • School: name, full address, board affiliation or recognition number, and a contact for fee queries.
  • Receipt: a unique, sequential number, the payment date, the academic year and the period covered (month, quarter or term).
  • Student and payer: student's full name, class, section and admission number, plus the parent or guardian who actually paid.
  • Fee heads: each head on its own line, with tuition separate. Show concessions and late fees as their own lines too.
  • Payment: total in figures and words, the mode (cash, cheque, UPI, card, net banking), the transaction/UPI reference or cheque number, and any balance outstanding.
  • Authentication: a signature and stamp on printed receipts, or a "computer-generated receipt" note on digital ones.

Sample school fee receipt layout

Here's a simple format you can adapt. The figures are placeholders.

[School Name] [Full address] · Affiliation No. [XXXXXXX] · Phone [XXXXXXXXXX]

FEE RECEIPT

Field Details
Receipt No. 2026-27/000482
Date of payment 15/07/2026
Academic year / Period 2026-27 / Term 2 (Jul–Sep)
Student name [Child's full name]
Class & section / Admission No. 6 – B / ADM-1234
Received from [Parent/guardian full name]
Fee head Amount (₹)
Tuition fee 12,000
Development fee 2,000
Examination fee 500
Transport fee 3,000
Less: Sibling concession (on tuition) (1,000)
Total paid 16,500
Payment details
Amount in words Rupees Sixteen Thousand Five Hundred Only
Mode UPI
Transaction / UPI reference [UTR / UPI Ref No.]
Balance outstanding ₹0

Authorised signatory / School stamp, or "This is a computer-generated receipt and does not require a signature."

If a concession applies only to tuition, label it that way, as above. Then the parent can see the net tuition paid was ₹11,000, not ₹12,000.

Itemising helps the school too. You get fewer reissue requests in the January–March declaration season and cleaner reconciliation by fee head. Year-end statements for parents are also easier to prepare.

Common mistakes on school fee receipts

  1. One lumped total. "School fees ₹16,500" is the biggest cause of repeat requests. Always itemise.
  2. Non-tuition charges labelled as tuition. Development fees and donations are excluded by law. Don't fold them or "annual charges" into the tuition line.
  3. Missing child's name or admission number. A receipt that names only the parent doesn't show which child it covers. That matters for families with several children.
  4. Wrong or missing payer. The parent who paid claims the deduction, so record the payer accurately.
  5. Duplicate receipt numbers. If you reissue a receipt, mark it "Duplicate copy" with the original number and date. Never create a second receipt for the same payment.
  6. A gateway screen used as a receipt. A UPI screenshot confirms money moved. It doesn't show fee heads.
  7. Academic year vs tax year. The tax year always runs 1 April to 31 March, whatever your academic calendar. A clear payment date on every receipt helps parents add up the right payments.
  8. Unnecessary ID details. We found no rule requiring the parent's PAN on a school receipt for this deduction, since employees give their PAN to the employer in Form 124. Avoid printing Aadhaar or other ID numbers without a clear reason.

Checklist for the school office

Before the next fee cycle, check that:

  • Every receipt shows school name, address and affiliation number
  • Receipt numbers are unique, sequential and never reused
  • The payment date is printed on every receipt
  • Student name, class and admission number appear on every receipt
  • The payer's name is recorded correctly
  • Tuition is its own fee head, separate from development, transport, exam and other charges
  • Concessions and late fees are shown as separate lines
  • Payment mode and transaction/UPI reference are captured
  • Total is shown in figures and words
  • Duplicates are clearly marked "Duplicate copy"
  • Digital receipts carry a "computer-generated" note
  • You can produce a year-end (April–March) summary per child on request

FAQs

What must a school fee receipt show?

A school fee receipt should show the school's name, address and affiliation number, a unique receipt number, the payment date, the student's name, class and admission number, the payer's name, the fee period, each fee head with its amount (tuition separately), the total, the payment mode and transaction reference, and a signature or computer-generated note.

Which school fees can parents claim under Section 80C (now Section 123)?

Only tuition fees paid for the full-time education of up to two children at a school, college, university or other educational institution in India. Development fees, donations and similar payments are excluded, and other non-tuition charges such as transport are generally not eligible. The claim falls within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit and is available only under the old tax regime.

Is Section 80C still valid in 2026-27?

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. The tuition fee deduction continues under Section 123 and Schedule XV, with the same two-child limit and ₹1.5 lakh overall ceiling. Section 80C still applies to income earned up to 31 March 2026.

What fee receipt does an employer need for Form 12BB or Form 124?

Form 12BB has been renumbered Form No. 124 for tax year 2026-27 onwards. Employers generally want a receipt or annual statement that names the child and school, shows the tuition amount separately and shows payment dates within the tax year (April–March). Each employer sets its own proof rules.

Can parents claim tuition fees under the new tax regime?

No. Under the default new tax regime, the Section 80C/Section 123 deduction, including tuition fees, is not allowed. Parents must choose the old tax regime to claim it. Schools should still issue itemised receipts, because many parents use the old regime.

Is a UPI payment screenshot enough as a fee receipt?

No. A UPI screenshot only confirms that money moved. It doesn't show the student, fee period or fee heads, so it can't show how much was tuition. Schools should issue an itemised receipt for every payment, including online ones.

This article is for general information only and isn't tax advice. Tax rules can change. Parents should confirm their eligibility and their employer's proof requirements with a qualified tax adviser.

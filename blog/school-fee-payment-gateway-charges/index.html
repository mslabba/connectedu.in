Who Pays the Payment Gateway Charge on School Fees? Parent Convenience Fee vs School-Absorbed, UPI's 0% MDR and What to Tell Parents

27 Oct 2026 · Fee collection · ConnectEdu Team

Book a Demo School Login

School fee payment gateway charges are the fees a bank or payment gateway takes on each online fee payment. A school can pass them to parents as a "convenience fee", absorb them as a cost, or split them by payment method. None of these is wrong. What causes trouble is a charge parents only discover on the checkout screen.

Most of the confusion starts with UPI. Standard UPI carries 0% MDR, yet many schools still see a charge on UPI fee payments. Here is where that charge comes from, how GST applies, the three ways schools handle it, and what to tell parents before they pay.

What UPI's 0% MDR actually covers

MDR (merchant discount rate) is the percentage a merchant pays on each digital payment it receives. Since 1 January 2020, section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 has said that no bank or system provider shall impose any charge on a payer or a beneficiary for the electronic modes prescribed under section 269SU of the Income-tax Act. CBDT Circular No. 32/2019 spells out what follows: "any charge including the MDR" does not apply to payments made through those modes. CBDT's Circular No. 16/2020 lists the modes notified on 30 December 2019: RuPay debit cards, UPI (BHIM-UPI) and UPI QR codes.

So why does your gateway statement still show a deduction on UPI fees? Razorpay's payment gateway pricing guide for schools (9 July 2026) explains it plainly:

  • Zero MDR is not zero cost. For a standard UPI payment from a bank account, MDR is 0%. But a gateway "may apply a platform fee of around 2% plus 18% GST on UPI transactions" for infrastructure, dashboards, reconciliation and reporting.
  • Not every UPI payment is the standard kind. Razorpay says UPI payments made with a RuPay credit card attract an MDR of roughly 1.1% to 2%, because a credit line is involved.
  • Some methods cost more. It says premium methods such as EMI, corporate cards and Pay Later are typically priced at 3% plus GST.

The same guide lists seven charge types to check before signing: MDR, TDR, convenience fees, platform fees, AMC, setup fees and technology fees. Ask your gateway in writing which of these apply to your account, and whether its platform fee applies to UPI.

How GST applies to the gateway's charge

GST is charged on the gateway's fee, not on the school fee itself. Razorpay's worked example: on a ₹100 payment at a 2% rate, the fee is ₹2 plus ₹0.36 GST, so ₹2.36 in total.

At that same illustrative rate, a ₹25,000 term fee paid online would carry ₹500 in gateway fee plus ₹90 GST, or ₹590. Your actual rate depends on your agreement, the payment method and any negotiated pricing. Razorpay, for example, says custom pricing is available above ₹5 lakh a month in collections. But the arithmetic shows why parents notice, and why trustees ask who is paying.

Two more costs are easy to miss. Razorpay notes that the gateway's fee on a payment may not be recovered when you refund it. And if you pass charges to parents, how the extra amount is shown on the receipt and in the accounts is a question for your auditor, not your fee clerk.

Three ways schools handle the charge

1. Parent pays (convenience fee)

The gateway adds its charge to the amount the parent pays, and the school receives the full fee. Some school software sets it up this way by default. The NextOS help center, for instance, says the "fee bearer" is set as the parent by default, with charges based on the payment mode the parent picks. A school that wants to bear the charges itself has to send an official email request to have the setting changed.

  • For: the school's fee income is exactly what the fee structure says, and the cost of a pricier method falls on whoever chooses it.
  • Against: parents read it as an extra fee. Razorpay warns that passing a flat convenience fee to payers "can be regressive", and it can push some parents back to the counter.

2. School absorbs the charge

The parent pays the exact fee, and the gateway deducts its charge from what the school receives.

  • For: no checkout surprises, the cleanest parent experience, and the strongest push towards online payment.
  • Against: it becomes a real cost line that grows with your online collections. Budget it, and report it to trustees, rather than discovering it in the bank reconciliation.

3. Split by payment method

Absorb the charge on low-cost methods and pass it on for expensive ones. This follows how charges actually vary. NextOS's explanation of convenience charges says credit cards generally carry higher transaction fees, while bank transfers or UPI "may have lower or zero fees".

  • For: parents always have a no-extra-cost route, and the school doesn't subsidise card rewards or EMI.
  • Against: the rules need explaining once, clearly, before the first due date.

A fourth option: ask your bank

Before choosing, talk to the bank that holds the school's account. In a case study published by Vawsum, a trust-run school in South 24 Parganas with more than 2,000 students moved fee collection online after its bank, a national private-sector bank, agreed to full subvention and waived the payment gateway charges entirely. The school reached 100% online collection within three months. Some parents kept paying at the counter in the early weeks, so the new route sat alongside the old one rather than replacing it on day one.

Not every bank will do that. But the school's banker is a better place to start than the gateway's standard rate card.

What to tell parents before they pay

Whichever model you pick, parent complaints come from surprise, not from the amount. A disclosure checklist for the accounts office:

  1. Put it in the fee circular. State at the start of the session which payment methods are free to the parent, which carry a charge, and roughly how much.
  2. Show the charge before payment, not after. Parents should see the fee, the charge and the total before they confirm. NextOS, for example, gives parents a View Charges option below the payment section before they proceed.
  3. Keep a no-charge route open. If you pass charges on, say clearly which method costs the parent nothing extra, whether that is standard UPI or paying at the counter.
  4. Show the fee and the charge separately on the receipt. A parent claiming tuition fees for tax purposes, or checking against the approved fee structure, needs the fee amount on its own. Our fee receipt format guide covers what a receipt should show.
  5. Decide the refund rule in advance. If a fee is refunded, say whether the convenience charge is refunded too. The gateway may not return its own fee to the school.
  6. Brief the front office. Give them a two-line answer to "why was I charged extra?" so every parent hears the same explanation.

Questions to ask your gateway or bank before you sign

  • Is there a platform fee on standard UPI, and at what rate?
  • What are the rates for credit cards, RuPay credit cards on UPI, corporate cards, EMI and net banking?
  • Is GST charged on top of these rates, and is it shown on the invoice?
  • Is there a setup fee, AMC or technology fee?
  • Can the charge be passed to the parent, absorbed by the school, or set differently by payment method, and who changes that setting?
  • What happens to the charge when a fee is refunded?
  • At what monthly volume is custom pricing available, and would the bank consider subvention?

Frequently asked questions

Is UPI free for school fee payments?

Standard UPI has 0% MDR. Under section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, banks and system providers cannot charge MDR on it. But a payment gateway may still charge the school a platform fee for its service. Razorpay's school pricing guide puts this at around 2% plus 18% GST, so check your own agreement.

Can a school charge parents a convenience fee for paying fees online?

Many schools pass gateway charges to parents, and some school software does this by default. Whether it suits your school is a policy decision for the management and auditors. Whatever you decide, show the charge before payment and keep a no-extra-cost option available.

Is GST charged on payment gateway charges?

Yes. GST at 18% applies to the gateway's fee. On a ₹100 payment at 2%, Razorpay's example works out to ₹2 plus ₹0.36 GST, so ₹2.36. GST is on the gateway's charge, not on the school fee.

Why do card payments cost more than UPI?

Standard UPI has 0% MDR, but credit cards do not. NextOS notes that credit cards generally carry higher transaction fees. Razorpay prices premium methods such as corporate cards, EMI and Pay Later at around 3% plus GST. That is why some schools pass on card charges but absorb UPI, or leave UPI as the no-charge option for parents.

Who should pay the gateway charge, the parent or the school?

There is no single answer. Absorbing it gives parents the smoothest experience but adds a cost that grows with online collections. Passing it on protects fee income but needs clear disclosure. Splitting by payment method is a common middle path. Ask your bank about subvention before you decide.

APAAR ID for Schools: How to Generate It, Fix Mismatches and Handle Consent

13 Nov 2026 · Student records · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

If your district office has started sending weekly APAAR numbers, you are not alone. On 6 November 2026 Madhya Pradesh's data showed 28.48 lakh students still waiting for an APAAR ID, with private schools at 77.8% coverage against 83.3% for government schools. District review meetings in Bihar and elsewhere are asking schools to finish the job "on priority".

This post explains the APAAR ID for schools in plain terms: what it is, how a school generates it, why so many records fail, what the 2026 Supreme Court directions changed about consent, and what the school office should keep on file. It is written for principals, office staff and UDISE+ coordinators in Indian schools.

What an APAAR ID is

APAAR stands for Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. It is a 12-digit permanent student ID under the Ministry of Education's "One Nation, One Student ID" programme, launched in line with NEP 2020. The idea is that a student's marksheets, certificates and later their higher-education credits sit against one number for life.

A few facts that matter for a school office:

  • It is generated through UDISE+, against the student's PEN (Permanent Education Number) already held there. No PEN, no APAAR ID.
  • It lands in DigiLocker. Once created, the APAAR card appears in the Issued Documents section of the student's DigiLocker account. Status can also be checked in the APAAR module of UDISE+.
  • It is not the same as Aadhaar. Aadhaar is used to verify identity while generating it. The APAAR ID is an education record ID.
  • It follows the child. When a student moves school, the same ID should move with them, which is why the details behind it need to be right the first time.

The APAAR portal is the official reference, and its helpline is 1800-889-3511.

How a school generates APAAR IDs

The official process has four steps, and the school does most of the work:

  1. Verify the student's details in UDISE+: name, date of birth, gender, mobile number, mother's and father's names, name as per Aadhaar and Aadhaar number.
  2. Collect parental consent, because most school students are minors.
  3. Authenticate the student's identity against Aadhaar.
  4. Generate the ID, which is then added to the student's DigiLocker.

In practice the bottleneck is almost never step 4. It is steps 1 and 2: details that do not match, and consent forms that were never collected or never filed.

Why APAAR generation fails

Most failures come down to the school's student record and the Aadhaar record saying different things. The common ones:

Problem What it looks like Who fixes it
Name mismatch "Mohammed" on admission register, "Muhammed" on Aadhaar; initials expanded in one and not the other School corrects UDISE+ if the school record is wrong; parent updates Aadhaar if Aadhaar is wrong
Date of birth mismatch Admission form copied from an old birth certificate; Aadhaar has a different date Decide which document is correct before changing anything
No PEN Student added late or transferred in without a UDISE+ profile School completes the UDISE+ student profile first
No Aadhaar, or Aadhaar not updated Young children whose biometrics were never updated Parent, often through an Aadhaar camp the school or block arranges
No consent on file Form sent home, never returned, or returned and lost Class teacher follows up; office files the signed form
Parent refuses Parent does not want the ID created Record the refusal properly (see below)

The fastest route through a backlog is to sort pending students into these groups first, then work each group as a batch, instead of trying one child at a time from the top of the list.

Consent after the 2026 Supreme Court directions

Consent is not a formality. Schools were told to collect signed, physical consent from parents, usually at a parent-teacher meeting, and CBSE's January 2025 circular said the same for affiliated schools.

In July 2026, as reported in legal commentary on Abhishek Baxi v Union of India, the Supreme Court directed that the model consent form be amended so parents have an express option to refuse, and that data handled under APAAR be governed by the DPDP Act, 2023. Personal information collected under the scheme cannot be shared with private entities except as the law allows.

What this means for the school office:

  • Use the current consent form from the APAAR portal's resources page, and check it again before each consent drive. A November 2026 explainer could not confirm that a revised national form had been published yet.
  • Explain, do not pressure. Tell parents what the ID is for and that they can say no. A consent collected under pressure is a problem for the school later.
  • File every form, including refusals. A signed "no" is a record, not a failure.
  • Do not reuse Aadhaar for anything else. The Aadhaar number collected for APAAR is for APAAR. Keep it out of WhatsApp groups and shared spreadsheets.

CBSE schools: APAAR and the List of Candidates

CBSE linked APAAR IDs to Class X and XII registration data for the 2025-26 examinations. After schools reported mismatches and missing consent, the Board allowed three kinds of entry for that session:

  • The 12-digit APAAR ID for students who have one
  • REFUSED where parents refused consent, with the school keeping a copy of the refusal
  • NOGEN where the ID could not be generated for other reasons

That relaxation was issued for 2025-26. For the current session, read CBSE's latest LOC circular rather than assume the same categories apply. Our CBSE LOC checklist and the guide to CBSE name and date-of-birth corrections cover the matching problems that also break APAAR generation.

What the school office should keep

You do not need a new register for APAAR. You need four things, kept against each student's record so they can be found in a minute:

  1. The APAAR ID itself (or a clear status: pending, refused, not generated, and why)
  2. The Aadhaar number and the name exactly as on Aadhaar, stored securely and visible only to staff who need it
  3. The signed consent or refusal form, with the date it came back
  4. A note of any correction made to name, date of birth or parent details, and on what document it was based

Keep this in the same student record your office already uses for admission, fees and attendance, not in a separate spreadsheet that only one person can open. If you are not sure what that should look like, see what a single student record is. The same IDs are also asked for in other claims; private schools filing for RTE fee reimbursement are now being asked for Aadhaar and APAAR details per child.

A one-week plan to clear your backlog

If you have a pending list from UDISE+ or the district, this is a realistic week:

  • Day 1: Download the pending list. Sort every student into one of the six groups in the table above.
  • Day 2: Class teachers check name and date of birth against the admission form and Aadhaar copy for their section. Mark which side is wrong.
  • Day 3: Office corrects school-side errors in UDISE+. Send parents a short note listing what needs updating on Aadhaar and where the nearest update camp is.
  • Day 4: Consent follow-up. Send the current form home again, or call parents in for a short meeting. Record refusals.
  • Day 5: Generate IDs for every student now clear. Update each student's record with the ID or status. Report numbers to the district.

Repeat for the stragglers the next week. Most schools find the second round is a fraction of the first.

Frequently asked questions

Is the APAAR ID compulsory for students?

Generating it needs parental consent for minors, and in July 2026 the Supreme Court directed that the model consent form include an option to refuse. CBSE allowed schools to enter REFUSED for students whose parents denied consent in 2025-26, with the school keeping a copy of the refusal. Check your board's and state's current instructions.

Who generates the APAAR ID, the school or the parent?

The school generates it through the APAAR module of UDISE+, against the student's PEN, after verifying details and collecting parental consent. The ID then appears in the student's DigiLocker.

Why does APAAR generation fail for some students?

Usually because the name, date of birth or gender in UDISE+ does not match Aadhaar, or because the student has no PEN or no valid Aadhaar. The record has to be corrected on the wrong side and resubmitted.

What should a school keep for each student?

The APAAR ID or its status, the Aadhaar number and name as on Aadhaar (stored securely), the signed consent or refusal form, and a note of any correction made and the document it was based on.

Can a school management system generate APAAR IDs?

Not in ConnectEdu's case. APAAR IDs are generated on the government UDISE+ system. ConnectEdu stores and exports the APAAR and Aadhaar details on each student's record and logs edits to student details.

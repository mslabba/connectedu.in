Student Promotion and Academic Year Rollover: A Checklist for the School Office

23 Nov 2026 · School Office · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

Every school runs the same rush between the last exam and the first day of the new session. Results go out, children move up a class, some stay back, some leave with a TC, Class 12 passes out, and fees for the new year must be ready before parents start paying. If promotion is done in a hurry, the mistakes show up in June: a child in the wrong section, last year's dues missing, attendance starting in the old class list, or a UDISE+ progression entry that does not match your own records.

This is a checklist for the school office to promote students and roll over the academic year cleanly. It works whatever software you use. At the end we explain where ConnectEdu fits.

Why year-end rollover goes wrong

Most rollover problems come from doing steps in the wrong order:

  • Promoting before results are final. A re-exam or a revaluation changes the outcome after the class has already moved.
  • Creating the new year after promotion. Students land in classes and sections that do not exist yet, or in last year's sections.
  • Treating everyone the same. A batch promotion moves the whole class, including the child who should be detained or who has already left.
  • Forgetting the money. Pending dues from last year are not visible to the accounts team once the new fee structure is assigned.
  • Leaving UDISE+ for later. UDISE+ asks schools to finish progression for the previous year before new entries. Delhi's Samagra Shiksha issued a circular on 24 July 2026 about pending student progression for 2025-26, months after that session ended.

Step 1: Close the current year first

Before anyone is promoted, lock down what the year produced:

  1. Finalise results. Marks entered, report cards checked and published, re-exams and revaluations settled.
  2. Close attendance. Total working days and days attended for each student. You will need this for UDISE+ and for any attendance shortage case.
  3. Settle the ledger as far as possible. Send a final dues reminder and export a list of each student's pending dues before anyone is promoted.
  4. List who is leaving. Students who have asked for a TC, and the Class 10 or 12 students who will not continue with you.
  5. Back up or export the year's records. Class lists, marks and fee ledgers for the year should be easy to pull out later.

Step 2: Set up the new academic year

Create the new year before you move anyone into it:

  • New classes and sections, with section strength and class teachers
  • Subjects and electives for each class (especially Class 9 and Class 11, where choices change)
  • The new fee structure: fee types, terms and instalments, assigned to classes
  • Concessions and discounts that continue, such as sibling or staff-ward discounts, reviewed rather than copied blindly
  • The timetable and exam plan can follow later, but sections must exist first

Step 3: Decide each student's outcome

Every student should have one clear outcome before promotion runs. A simple list per class, signed off by the class teacher and the principal, is enough:

Outcome What happens next
Promoted Moves to the next class
Detained / repeats Stays in the same class for the new year
Promoted after re-exam Moves up once the re-exam result is in
Left with TC Moves out of active students; records kept
Passed out (Class 10 or 12, not continuing) Moves out of active students, into alumni records

UDISE+ uses similar categories in its progression activity, including promoted with or without examination, not promoted or repeater, and left school with or without TC. Using the same outcomes in your own list makes the UDISE+ entry much faster.

Detention in Classes 5 and 8

Detention is no longer automatic or banned everywhere. In December 2024 the Centre amended the RTE rules so states can detain students in Classes 5 and 8. Under the amended rules, a child who does not meet the promotion criteria gets extra teaching and a re-examination within two months of the result. Only if the child does not pass the re-exam is the child kept back.

States have made different choices, and CBSE schools follow the decision of the state they are in. So before you mark anyone "detained" in Class 5 or 8:

  • Check your state's current rule.
  • Keep the re-exam date and result on the student's record.
  • Do not finalise Class 5 and 8 promotion until the re-exam window is over.

Step 4: Promote class by class

With outcomes decided, promotion itself is quick:

  1. Start from the top class and work down, so sections free up in order and you can see seat numbers.
  2. Batch-promote the class, leaving out the students marked detained, left or passed out.
  3. Handle exceptions individually. Detained students stay; students promoted after a re-exam are moved when the result is in.
  4. Reassign sections where needed. Section changes, new electives and new admissions joining existing sections.
  5. Check the counts. Students in the new class should equal promoted students plus repeaters plus new admissions. If the numbers do not match, stop and find out why before the session starts.

Students who leave, pass out or transfer

Students who leave should not disappear from your records. You will be asked for their details again: a duplicate TC, a CEA or bonafide certificate for the year they studied, verification from a college, or an old fee receipt.

  • Mark the outcome and the date of leaving on the student's record.
  • Keep their marks, attendance and fee history for each year they studied.
  • Move passed-out students into alumni records so the school can still reach them.

Fees: what carries over

Money is where rollover mistakes cost the most:

  • Pending dues from last year may not move up with the child automatically. Export the pending-dues list before promotion and add each balance to the student's new-year fees, so no old balance is quietly dropped.
  • The new fee structure should be assigned by class after promotion, so each child gets the right fees for the class they are now in.
  • Advance payments made for the new year before promotion should be checked once the child is in the new class.
  • Concessions should be reviewed, not copied. A sibling discount may end when an elder child passes out.

UDISE+ student progression

UDISE+ has its own progression step. In the SDMS school login, under Student Movement and Progression, the school selects each previous-year class and section and records each student's progression status, marks in percentage where applicable, days attended, and whether the student continues in the same school or has left. Each class is then finalised, and corrections remain possible after that.

If your own year-end list already holds the outcome, percentage and days attended for every student, the UDISE+ entry becomes copying, not hunting through registers. See our guide to APAAR ID for schools for the student ID side of the same data.

A simple timeline

When What
February Plan sections, subjects and fee structure for the new year
March Final exams; attendance closed; dues reminder sent
End of March / early April Results published; outcome list per class signed off
April New year set up; class-by-class promotion; new fees assigned
Within two months of results Class 5 and 8 re-exams where your state detains; final promotion
After promotion UDISE+ progression completed and finalised

Your dates will vary with your board and state. The order matters more than the dates.

Frequently asked questions

When should a school promote students to the next class?

After results are final and every student's outcome is decided, and after the new academic year's classes and sections are set up. For Classes 5 and 8 in states that detain, wait until the re-exam results are in.

Can we promote the whole class at once?

Yes, batch promotion is the fastest way. Leave out students who are detained, have left, or are passing out, and handle them one by one.

What happens to a student's old fee dues after promotion?

Many systems do not carry them over automatically. Export the pending-dues list before you promote, then add each balance to the student's new-year fees so the accounts team can still see it.

Are Class 5 and Class 8 students still automatically promoted?

Not everywhere. Since the December 2024 RTE rules amendment, states can detain students in Classes 5 and 8 after a re-examination within two months of the result. Check your state's rule.

Do we have to update UDISE+ after promotion?

Yes. UDISE+ asks schools to complete and finalise student progression for the previous year, class by class, before moving on to the new year's entries.

What should we do with students who pass out?

Mark them as passed out with the date, keep their full record, and move them into alumni records. Certificate and verification requests will keep coming for years.

What Is a Single Student Record? Why One Profile per Child Beats Five Registers

30 Oct 2026 · School management · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

A single student record is one profile for each child that every part of the school uses: admissions, fees, attendance, exams, transport and messages to parents. A fact is entered once, in one place, and everyone who needs it sees the same version. When a parent's phone number changes or a fee is paid, it changes once, not in five separate books.

Most Indian schools do not have a data problem. They have a copies problem. The same child is written into the admission register, the fee ledger, the class attendance register, a marks spreadsheet, the bus list and a WhatsApp group, and each copy slowly drifts away from the others. This guide explains what a single student record is, what goes into it, and how a school can move to one without stopping the office for a month.

Why schools end up with five versions of one child

Every register was started for a good reason. The office needed an admission register, accounts needed a fee ledger, class teachers needed attendance, the exam in-charge needed a marks sheet, and the transport head needed a stop-wise bus list. Nobody planned for them to disagree. They disagree because nobody is responsible for keeping them in step.

You can usually spot it in a few everyday problems:

  • A parent changes their mobile number at the office, but absence messages and fee reminders still go to the old one.
  • A fee is paid at the counter, and the family still gets a reminder the next morning.
  • A child's name is spelt one way in the admission register and another way on the report card.
  • A student moves to a new bus stop, and the driver's list is still the old one at the end of the month.
  • The principal asks how many Class 9 students were absent more than five days this term, and someone spends an afternoon counting.

None of these is a big failure on its own. Together they cost staff hours every week and wear down the trust parents have in what the school tells them.

What goes into one student record

A complete student record usually brings together:

  1. Identity and family. Name, date of birth, photo, parent or guardian names and contacts, and the government IDs the school keeps for the child.
  2. Enrolment. Admission number, class, section, roll number, admission date and subjects.
  3. Fees. The fee structure that applies, instalments, payments, receipts and anything still due.
  4. Attendance. Daily attendance and leave.
  5. Academics. Marks, exam results and report cards.
  6. Transport. Route, stop and who picks the child up.
  7. Communication. Notices and alerts sent to the parent.
  8. History. Who changed what in the record, and when.

The point is not to collect more information about children. It is to enter each fact once and use it everywhere it is needed.

Registers and spreadsheets vs one shared record

Situation Separate registers and spreadsheets One shared student record
A child is absent Teacher marks the register. The office may call the parent later, if someone remembers. Attendance is marked once and the parent can be alerted from the same record.
Parent changes phone number Updated in the office file only. Updated once, so every later message uses the new number.
Fee paid at the counter Entered in the ledger. Nobody else knows until the next reconciliation. The payment and receipt sit on the child's record, visible to the office and the parent.
Term marks Typed into a spreadsheet, then retyped into report cards. Entered once, and the report card comes from the same marks.
A parent disputes a mark or a fee Staff search several files to work out what happened. The record shows the entry and its edit history.
Principal wants a number Someone counts by hand. The answer comes from the record in minutes.

Why it matters more in 2026

Three government changes make one clean record per child more useful than ever.

UDISE+ now works child by child. The Ministry of Education's UDISE+ system collects student-level data from schools, and each student gets a unique ID in it. Schools that keep student details in several places end up correcting the same child in several places before they can submit.

APAAR IDs depend on matching details. APAAR, the Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry under the "One Nation, One Student ID" programme, gives each student a 12-digit ID that follows them across schools. Creating it needs parental consent and details that match the child's official documents. Schools have found that a name or date of birth that differs between registers holds the process up. We covered the CBSE side of this in CBSE name and DOB corrections.

Children's data has stricter rules. Under Section 9 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, organisations must get verifiable consent from a parent before processing a child's personal data. It is much easier to know what you hold, who can see it and who changed it when it lives in one record than when copies sit in exercise books, laptops and phones. This is general information, not legal advice, so check the current rules with your adviser.

How to move to a single student record without stopping the office

You do not have to switch everything in one weekend. A staged move works better:

  1. Clean the master list first. Pick one source, usually the admission register, and check every child's name, date of birth, class, section and parent contacts against documents. Fix spellings now, before anything else is built on top.
  2. Agree who owns each field. The office owns identity and contacts, accounts owns fees, class teachers own attendance, the exam in-charge owns marks. Write it down.
  3. Move fees and attendance next. These change every day and cause the most disputes, so they gain the most from sitting on one record.
  4. Then marks and report cards. Enter marks once and produce report cards from them.
  5. Then transport and the parent app. Once the record is reliable, parents can see the same version the office sees.
  6. Retire the old registers on a date. Set a date after which the old spreadsheet is read-only, so nobody keeps updating a copy out of habit.
  7. Check the edit history monthly. Look at who changed marks, fees and attendance, so problems are spotted early.

What to ask any school software vendor

When you look at a school management system, ask the vendor to show you, on screen with sample data:

  • One child's profile with fees, attendance, marks, transport and parent contacts in one place.
  • What happens elsewhere when you change a parent's phone number once.
  • What a class teacher can see compared with the accounts office and a parent.
  • The history of who edited a mark or a fee entry, and when.
  • How you would export your school's data if you ever left.

Frequently asked questions

What is a single student record in a school?

It is one profile for each child that every part of the school uses, including admissions, fees, attendance, exams, transport and parent communication. Each fact is entered once and everyone sees the same version.

What is an SIS in a school?

SIS stands for student information system, the part of school software that holds student profiles, enrolment, attendance and results. In India, a school ERP usually includes the SIS along with fees, transport and other operations.

Is a single student record the same as a school ERP?

Not quite. A school ERP is the software. A single student record is a way the software is built, where every module links to the same profile for each child rather than keeping its own copy. Some systems have many modules but still keep separate copies.

Do we need to move all our data at once?

No. Clean the master list first, then move fees and attendance, then marks, then transport and the parent app. Set a date to stop updating the old registers.

How does one record help with UDISE+ and APAAR?

Both depend on student details that match official documents. When a child's name, date of birth and parent details are kept in one place and corrected once, there are fewer mismatches to chase before submissions.

Who should be able to change a student record?

Only the role that owns each field, such as the office for contacts and the exam in-charge for marks, and every change should be logged with who made it and when.

Cloud or On-Premise School ERP: Who Runs the Server, Backups and Updates

18 Nov 2026 · School ERP · Muhammed Shihabudeen Labba

Book a Demo School Login

Some schools still run their ERP on a computer in the office. Others log in to software that runs in a data centre somewhere else. Both can work. The difference is who looks after the server, the backups and the updates, and what happens to your fee records and marks on the day something breaks.

This guide compares cloud vs on-premise school ERP in plain terms for principals, trustees and school IT in-charges in India: what each one means, who does which job, what the DPDP Rules expect from 13 May 2027, when keeping a server on campus still makes sense, and the questions to put to any vendor before you sign.

Cloud and on-premise: what the words mean

An on-premise school ERP is installed on a server or desktop that the school owns, usually in the office or a staff room. The school, or a local vendor on an annual maintenance contract, keeps it running.

A cloud school ERP runs on servers in a data centre. Office staff, teachers and parents reach it through a browser or an app, and the vendor runs the machines, the backups and the software updates.

A third pattern sits in between: the software is on the school's own server, but staff reach it from outside through remote access. It looks like the cloud to users, but the school still owns every job that comes with the server.

Who does which job

The choice is mostly about who carries each of these jobs. Read the table as "who has to remember to do this".

Job On-premise Cloud
Buying and replacing the server School Vendor
Power backup and cooling for the server School Vendor's data centre
Taking backups and storing a copy off site School Vendor (ask how often)
Testing that a backup actually restores School Vendor (ask for evidence)
Security patches and software updates School or AMC vendor Vendor
Remote access for the principal, parents and branches School has to set it up Built in through browser and apps
Deciding who can see which records School School, using the vendor's role settings
Keeping working when the internet is down Office can, apps cannot Needs a connection

Two jobs never move to the vendor, whichever you choose: deciding which staff see which records, and being answerable to parents for how their children's data is used.

Backups: the question that decides it

Most schools say they have backups. Far fewer can say when they last restored one and checked that the fee ledger and marks came back complete.

With an on-premise server, a typical backup is a copy to an external drive, often kept in the same room as the server. A fire, a theft, a power surge or ransomware can take both together. A sound routine needs:

  • a daily backup that runs without anyone remembering to start it;
  • at least one copy kept away from the campus;
  • a test restore every term, so you know the copy is usable;
  • a named person who checks it, and a written note each time.

With a cloud ERP, the vendor does this work. That does not mean you skip the question. Ask how often backups are taken, where the copies are kept, how long a restore takes, and whether they have done one recently.

Security and the DPDP Rules

From 13 May 2027, a school handling students' personal data has to meet the security rules under the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025. Rule 6 of the DPDP Rules (G.S.R. 846(E), MeitY PDF) sets a minimum that includes:

  • encryption, masking or similar measures to protect the data;
  • control over who can access the computers that hold it;
  • logs, monitoring and review of access, so unauthorised access can be detected and investigated;
  • measures to keep working if data is lost or damaged, "such as by way of data-backups";
  • keeping those logs for one year, unless another law asks for longer;
  • a contract with each data processor that requires these safeguards.

These apply whether the server is in your office or in a data centre. On-premise, the school has to do every item itself: encrypting the disk, keeping access logs, keeping them for a year. On cloud, most of the technical work falls to the vendor, but the school still has to check it is done and have it in the contract. Our DPDP Act guide for schools covers consent, children's data and the vendor questions in more detail.

A server in a staff room is not automatically safer because you can see it. What matters is whether it is patched, whether its passwords are shared, and whether anyone would notice an unusual login.

Internet, power and the school office

The strongest argument for on-premise used to be "we can work when the internet is down". That still holds for the office computers connected to the server. It does not help parents paying fees from home, teachers marking attendance on a phone, or a bus tracking app, because all of those need the internet anyway.

For a cloud ERP, the practical fix is simple:

  • a second internet connection in the office, such as a mobile hotspot or a different broadband provider;
  • a UPS for the router as well as the computers;
  • a paper fallback for the few tasks that cannot wait, such as a manual fee receipt book used only during outages and entered later.

When on-premise still makes sense

Keeping the ERP on campus is a reasonable choice when:

  • your trust, management or a funding body requires student data to stay on campus;
  • you have a capable IT person and a backup routine that has been tested;
  • the school does not need parent apps, online fee payment, live bus tracking or access across branches.

If none of these applies, the case for buying and minding a server is weak. Many schools on on-premise systems are there because that was the only option when they started, not because they chose it recently.

Questions to ask before you choose

Put these to your own IT person first:

  1. When did we last restore our data from a backup, and did it work?
  2. Where is the backup copy kept, and is it off campus?
  3. Who is called if the server fails during fee week or result week?
  4. How many hours a month go into keeping the server running?
  5. Can parents and teachers use the system from their phones today?

Then put these to any cloud vendor, and ask for the answers in writing:

  1. Where is our data hosted, and is any of it processed outside India?
  2. How often are backups taken, and how long does a restore take?
  3. What uptime do you commit to, and what happens when you miss it?
  4. Can we limit what each staff role sees?
  5. Is there an audit log of who changed marks, fees or attendance?
  6. Will you sign a written data processing agreement?
  7. If we leave, can we export all our data, and will you delete it and confirm in writing?

The last question matters most. Whichever model you choose, the school's records should never be locked into one vendor.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between cloud and on-premise school ERP?

An on-premise school ERP runs on a server the school owns and keeps on campus, and the school looks after its hardware, backups, security and updates. A cloud school ERP runs in a vendor's data centre, staff and parents use it through a browser or app, and the vendor runs the servers, backups and updates.

Is cloud school ERP safe for student data?

It can be, if the vendor encrypts data, controls access, keeps logs and takes tested backups, and puts these in a written agreement. A server on campus is not safer just because it is nearby; it depends on whether it is patched, backed up off site and watched for unusual access.

Do the DPDP Rules require schools to use the cloud?

No. The DPDP Rules do not say where data must be hosted. From 13 May 2027 they require reasonable security safeguards, including encryption or similar measures, access control, access logs kept for a year, backups and contracts with data processors, wherever the data sits.

What happens to a cloud school ERP when the internet goes down?

The office cannot reach it until the connection is back. Most schools handle this with a second internet connection, such as a mobile hotspot, and a UPS for the router. Parents and teachers using apps are usually on mobile data anyway.

Can we get our data back if we leave a cloud ERP vendor?

You should be able to. Ask for data export and deletion on exit in writing before you sign. With ConnectEdu, a school can export or delete a student's data on request.
